What Is a Skill?

A Skill is a set of instructions you give to ChatGPT so it can do a specific job well — repeatedly — without you re-explaining the details every time.

Think of it like training a new colleague on a task. You explain the process once — what to ask, how to format the output, what rules to follow — and from then on, they just do it when the situation calls for it.

A Skill lives in your ChatGPT account (or in a shared workspace), and once it's there, ChatGPT can use it from any conversation. You don't need to open a special app or switch to a different tool. You talk to ChatGPT the way you normally would, and if your request matches a skill you've set up, it kicks in automatically.

Example: The Proposal Builder Skill

This skill knows our proposal structure, the questions to ask before drafting, and how we format the final document. When you tell ChatGPT "draft a proposal for this client," it asks you the specific questions it needs answered, then drafts the proposal in our format — no template pasting, no re-explaining our structure.

Key distinction

A prompt is a one-time instruction. A Skill is a reusable capability that's always available. You build it once, and it works every time the situation calls for it.

Why Skills Matter

How Skills Speed Up Work

Skills don't just save time on individual tasks — they compound. Multiple skills can fire in the same conversation, each one picking up where the last left off.

This is where skills go from "useful shortcut" to "this changes how I work." Here's a realistic example of what chaining looks like:

Chaining in action
  • "Take these notes from my client call and draft a proposal." → The Proposal Builder fires. It asks clarifying questions, then drafts in our template.
  • "Check the proposal for AI tells." → The AI Tells Editor scans against your curated list of patterns — not a generic humanizer.
  • "Run this by the Sales Psychologist." → The Sales Psychology skill reviews for persuasion, framing, and decision friction. Returns focused recommendations.

Three different skills, one conversation, one consistent thread of client context. No tool-switching, no re-explaining. ChatGPT carried the full context forward throughout.

Without skills

Start from scratch every time

Each conversation requires re-explaining your process, your format, and your preferences. You're essentially re-training ChatGPT on every request.

With skills

Pick up where you left off

Your processes are always loaded. You just say what you need, and ChatGPT already knows the how. Multiple skills can build on each other in a single conversation.

Why Skills Matter

Triggers: How Skills Activate

Every skill has two things that tell ChatGPT when to activate it: a description and triggers. Understanding how triggers work is the key to building skills that fire reliably.

There are three types of triggers, and they differ in reliability:

Trigger type How it works
Specific phrase You say "red team this," and the Red Teamer skill fires. Direct keyword match.
Direct instruction You say "draft a proposal for this client," and the Proposal Builder fires. Not a keyword — ChatGPT recognizes the instruction falls within the skill's job.
Inferred from context You paste raw meeting notes without saying anything, and ChatGPT recognizes the format and fires Meeting Debrief automatically.
Important

Context-based triggering is the least reliable — the hit rate is roughly 50% as of May 2026. But when your description includes imperative language ("Always use this skill when…" or "Never use this skill when…"), the hit rate jumps to 90%+. Write your descriptions with clear imperatives.

Why Skills Matter

Skills: Relevant Across Platforms

A skill is just a document — a set of instructions written in plain language. That means it's not locked to ChatGPT.

A skill you build for ChatGPT can also be uploaded to Claude, Gemini, or other AI platforms. The logic and instructions travel with it. You don't rebuild from scratch when you try a different tool — you upload the same document and it works.

Custom GPTs, by contrast, are locked to ChatGPT entirely. If you switch platforms, you start over.

Why this matters

AI tools are changing fast. Skills are a smarter long-term investment because you're building reusable building blocks, not platform-specific tools. The thinking you put into a skill — the process, the rules, the format — carries forward regardless of where the AI landscape goes.

What Skills Look Like in Practice

Skills aren't just one type of thing. Some handle a specific mechanical task. Others guide you through a workflow. Some push your thinking.

Tactical skills — they do the thing

📄

Doc Converter

Paste your content in. Get a properly formatted Word document out — fonts, headers, margins already applied.

📊

Presentation Creator

Turn a written draft into a deck without touching a slide. Structure and layout already handled.

🔍

AI Tells Editor

Scans writing for patterns that sound AI-generated and suggests targeted fixes. Checks against your own curated list, not a generic filter.

Role & workflow skills — they guide a process

📋

Proposal Builder

Start with client notes. End with a proposal in our format. Asks the right qualifying questions first.

📝

Meeting Debrief

Drop in your notes. Walk away with a structured summary, action items, owners, and deadlines.

Cross-cutting & thinking skills — they sharpen everything else

🎙️

My Voice

Any time something goes out with your name on it, this skill makes sure it sounds like you — not like ChatGPT.

🎯

Red Teamer

Find the holes in your argument before your client does. Stress-tests ideas for weaknesses and blind spots.

Pro tip

Start with a task you already do repeatedly and know well. If you can describe the process clearly to a new colleague, you can turn it into a skill.

Skills vs. Projects vs. Custom GPTs

This is a common source of confusion. The short version: use Skills for repeatable tasks, Projects for bodies of work, and Custom GPTs only when you need a standalone assistant with a large knowledge base.

Tool What it is Best for
Skills Reusable instructions that fire automatically in any conversation. Repeatable tasks you want done the same way every time, across any conversation or project.
Projects A dedicated workspace for a specific body of work — a client engagement, a course, a proposal process. Work that has its own uploaded files, reference materials, and ongoing context.
Custom GPTs A standalone AI assistant with its own personality, instructions, and knowledge base. Specialized assistants that need a large uploaded reference library. For most other tasks, Skills are the better choice.

Skills and Projects work well together. A Project gives you a workspace with files and context. Skills give you repeatable processes that fire within that workspace. You don't choose between them — you layer them.

When to reach for a Custom GPT

Custom GPTs still make sense when you need a standalone assistant with its own large reference library — think a knowledge base with dozens of uploaded documents. For most repeatable tasks, Skills are simpler, more flexible, and easier to maintain.
